# Seattle Seahawks: 5-Year Performance Review & Super Bowl LX Readiness

## Executive Summary

The Seattle Seahawks' journey from a 7-10 rebuilding season in 2021 to the NFC's #1 seed and Super Bowl LX appearance in 2025 is one of the most remarkable roster transformations in recent NFL history. Fueled by the Russell Wilson trade haul, a coaching change from Pete Carroll to Mike Macdonald, and aggressive 2025 free agency moves, the Seahawks enter Super Bowl LX as 4.5-point favorites over the New England Patriots.

---

## Five-Year Overview (2021-2025)

### Season-by-Season Records

| Season | Record | Postseason | Head Coach | QB |
|--------|--------|------------|------------|-----|
| 2021 | 7-10 | Missed playoffs | Pete Carroll | Russell Wilson |
| 2022 | 9-8 | Wild Card loss (41-23 vs SF) | Pete Carroll | Geno Smith |
| 2023 | 9-8 | Missed playoffs | Pete Carroll | Geno Smith |
| 2024 | 10-7 | Missed playoffs (tiebreaker) | Mike Macdonald | Geno Smith |
| 2025 | **14-3** | **NFC Champions / Super Bowl LX** | Mike Macdonald | Sam Darnold |

### 2021: The End of the Wilson Era (7-10)

Russell Wilson's final season in Seattle was marred by his first losing record as a starter and the first missed games of his career. Wilson suffered a broken middle finger on his throwing hand in Week 5 against the Rams, requiring surgery and missing three games. He finished with 3,113 passing yards, 25 touchdowns, and six interceptions. The 7-10 record was Seattle's worst since 2009 and signaled the end of an era. Despite the struggles, Wilson earned his ninth Pro Bowl nomination.

### 2022: The Geno Smith Surprise (9-8, Wild Card)

The blockbuster Russell Wilson trade to Denver on March 8, 2022 was the catalyst for Seattle's rebuild. The Seahawks received:
- **2022 1st-round pick** (used on OT Charles Cross, #9 overall)
- **2022 2nd-round pick** (used on EDGE Boye Mafe)
- **2022 5th-round pick**
- **2023 1st-round pick** (used on CB Devon Witherspoon, #5 overall)
- **2023 2nd-round pick** (used on WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba, #20 overall -- traded up)
- **TE Noah Fant, DE Shelby Harris, QB Drew Lock**

Geno Smith, who won the starting job in training camp, delivered one of the NFL's greatest comeback stories. He led the NFL in completion percentage, was named to his first Pro Bowl, and won NFL Comeback Player of the Year. The Seahawks made the playoffs at 9-8 but were eliminated 41-23 by the 49ers in the Wild Card round.

### 2023: Close But No Cigar (9-8, Missed Playoffs)

Seattle started 5-2 before injuries to Geno Smith, Kenneth Walker III, DK Metcalf, and Devon Witherspoon derailed the season. They lost five of their next six games. Despite a 9-8 finish (matching 2022), they missed the playoffs, finishing third in the NFC West behind San Francisco and Los Angeles. Smith set franchise records and led seven go-ahead fourth-quarter/overtime touchdowns, the most in a single NFL season ever. He was named to a second consecutive Pro Bowl.

### 2024: Macdonald's Foundation Year (10-7, Missed Playoffs)

Pete Carroll was let go on January 10, 2024 after 14 seasons, ending one of the most successful coaching tenures in franchise history. Mike Macdonald, the 36-year-old former Ravens defensive coordinator who had built Baltimore's historic 2023 defense, was hired on January 31, 2024, on a six-year deal, becoming the youngest head coach in the NFL.

Macdonald's first year produced a franchise-best 10 wins for a first-year coach. However, the Seahawks missed the playoffs by the narrowest of margins, losing to the Rams on the fifth tiebreaker (strength of victory) after both teams finished 10-7. The defense showed significant improvement in the second half of the season, foreshadowing the dominant 2025 unit.

## 2025 Season Deep Dive: A Franchise Transformation

### The Bold Offseason

The 2025 offseason was transformative. The Seahawks made several blockbuster moves:

- **Released Tyler Lockett** (March 5) -- saving $17M in cap space after his least productive season since 2017
- **Traded DK Metcalf to Pittsburgh** (March 13) -- received the 52nd overall pick in the 2025 Draft
- **Signed QB Sam Darnold** (March 13) -- 3-year, $100.5M contract ($55M guaranteed)
- **Signed WR Cooper Kupp** (March 14) -- 3-year, $45M contract ($17.5M guaranteed); the Yakima, Washington native returned to his home state after being released by the Rams

### Sam Darnold: The Redemption Story

Darnold, the No. 3 overall pick in 2018 who had bounced from the Jets to the Panthers to the Vikings, signed a prove-it-style deal with an easy out after Year 1. His performance exceeded all expectations:

**Regular Season Stats:**
- 4,048 passing yards (5th in NFL)
- 25 touchdowns / 14 interceptions
- QBR of 55.7 (19th)
- 2nd Pro Bowl selection

**Season Arc:** After a shaky start, Darnold caught fire by Week 3 and peaked with a brilliant shootout performance against the Buccaneers. His first half was outstanding (16 TDs, 5 INTs, 6-2 record), while the second half saw regression in individual stats (9 TDs, 9 INTs) even as the team went 8-1, carried by the elite defense.

**Playoff Performance:** Darnold elevated his game when it mattered most:
- 37-of-53 (69.8%), 470 yards, 4 TDs, 0 INTs in two playoff games
- Came through under pressure in the NFC Championship, throwing three touchdown passes including the go-ahead score to Cooper Kupp

### Jaxon Smith-Njigba: Offensive Player of the Year

The crown jewel of the Wilson trade haul, Smith-Njigba exploded in his third season:

- **1,793 receiving yards** (led NFL; shattered DK Metcalf's franchise record of 1,303)
- **119 receptions on 163 targets**
- **10 touchdowns**
- **542 deep receiving yards** (led NFL; 88 more than second place CeeDee Lamb)
- Named **2025 AP NFL Offensive Player of the Year** (272 points, 14 first-place votes) -- only the second Seahawk to win the award (Shaun Alexander, 2005)

### Cooper Kupp: The Veteran Complement

The former Super Bowl LVI MVP provided a reliable second option:
- Regular season: 47 catches, 593 yards, 2 TDs
- Playoffs: 9 receptions, 96 yards, 1 TD (including the go-ahead score in the NFC Championship)

### The NFL's Best Defense

Mike Macdonald's defense was the engine of Seattle's 2025 dominance:

**Key Rankings:**
- **1st in points allowed:** 292 total (17.2 per game)
- **2nd in rushing yards allowed:** 88.8 per game
- **2nd in net yards per pass attempt allowed**
- **1st in pressure rate:** 40.4% (one of the lightest blitzing defenses in the NFL)
- **1st in EPA per play against the run**
- **6th in takeaways**

**Defensive Stars:**
- **Devon Witherspoon (CB):** PFF's #1 overall graded CB (89.9), with elite marks in coverage (84.6), pass rush (91.8), and run defense (86.7). 72 tackles, 7 PBUs, 1 INT in 12 games. Third Pro Bowl in three seasons.
- **Leonard Williams (DT):** Second-team All-Pro. Combined with Byron Murphy III for the most QB pressures (108), QB hits (35), and sacks (14.0) among 300-pound duos in the NFL.
- **Boye Mafe (EDGE):** Top-20 in ESPN pass-rush win rate. Another Wilson trade dividend.
- **Nick Emmanwori (S):** The 20-year-old safety appeared in 14 games with 11 starts, playing a central role in the defensive scheme.
- **Uchenna Nwosu (EDGE):** Top-20 in ESPN pass-rush win rate alongside Mafe.

**The "Dark Side" Defense:** Seattle's 4.7% explosive-play differential was the best in the NFL and the ninth-best figure posted by any team in the past 25 years.

### Kenneth Walker III and the Ground Game

Walker played all 17 games: 221 carries, 1,027 yards, 5 TDs, 4.6 yards per carry. The rushing attack ranked 25th in yards per carry (4.0) but 4th in attempts (385), reflecting the team's commitment to establishing the run.

Walker's late-season surge was critical: in his last five games (including playoffs), he posted 80 carries for 426 yards (5.3 YPC), 5 TDs, and 28 first downs. His 116-yard, 3-TD performance in the divisional win over the 49ers was a signature moment.

### 14-3 Regular Season and NFC #1 Seed

The Seahawks captured:
- Their first NFC West title since 2020
- The NFC's #1 overall seed for the first time since 2014
- A franchise record for regular-season wins (14)
- A nine-game winning streak entering the Super Bowl

## 2025 Playoff Run

### NFC Divisional Round: Seahawks 41, 49ers 6

A complete demolition. Seattle dominated from the opening play (Rashid Shaheed returned the opening kickoff for a touchdown), forced six turnovers, and held San Francisco to just six points. Kenneth Walker rushed for 116 yards and 3 TDs. This was Seattle's first postseason win since 2019.

### NFC Championship: Seahawks 31, Rams 27

A thrilling, back-and-forth battle. Sam Darnold threw three touchdown passes, including the go-ahead score to Cooper Kupp in the fourth quarter. The Seahawks advanced to the Super Bowl for the first time since Super Bowl XLIX after the 2014 season.

## Super Bowl LX Readiness Assessment

### Injury Report (as of February 7, 2026)

The Seahawks are remarkably healthy heading into the Super Bowl:
- **Only 1 player with injury designation:** FB Robbie Ouzts (questionable, neck)
- **Sam Darnold** (oblique): full participant Friday
- **Charles Cross** (OT): upgraded to full participant
- **Nick Emmanwori** (S): cleared, no injury designation after ankle concern
- All other players healthy and available

### Strengths

1. **Elite defense** -- NFL's best scoring defense, first in pressure rate despite light blitzing
2. **JSN/Kupp receiving duo** -- The OPOY winner paired with a former Super Bowl MVP
3. **Darnold's playoff form** -- 0 INTs in two playoff games after 14 in the regular season
4. **Momentum** -- Nine-game winning streak, dominant playoff performances
5. **Health** -- Essentially full strength entering the game
6. **Coaching** -- Macdonald's defensive scheme has neutralized opponents all season
7. **Youth core from Wilson trade** -- Witherspoon, JSN, Cross, Mafe all performing at elite levels

### Weaknesses and Concerns

1. **Darnold's turnover history** -- 14 INTs in the regular season; his second-half regression (9 TDs, 9 INTs in the final 9 games) raises questions about consistency under pressure
2. **Rushing efficiency** -- 25th in yards per carry (4.0) despite high volume; can they run effectively against New England?
3. **Cooper Kupp's declining production** -- Only 47 catches and 593 yards in the regular season; health and age (32) are factors
4. **Playoff sample size** -- Just two games; the 49ers were a 6-seed and the Rams were underdogs
5. **Super Bowl experience** -- No players or coaches (besides OC Josh McDaniels) with Super Bowl experience on this roster

### Key Matchup Factors vs. New England Patriots

- **Explosive plays battle:** Seattle's 4.7% explosive-play differential (best in NFL) vs. New England's league-best explosive creation rate (13.6%). This is the defining tactical matchup.
- **Darnold's turnovers vs. Patriots' takeaway ability:** If Darnold reverts to his second-half turnover tendencies, New England could capitalize
- **Seattle's pass rush vs. Drake Maye:** The Seahawks' 40.4% pressure rate without blitzing could disrupt New England's passing game
- **Kenneth Walker's late-season surge:** If Seattle can run effectively, it keeps Darnold in favorable game scripts and avoids extended dropback situations

### Historical Context: The Super Bowl XLIX Rematch

This is the 10th Super Bowl rematch in history and the fourth involving the Patriots. In Super Bowl XLIX (February 1, 2015), the Patriots defeated the Seahawks 28-24 on Malcolm Butler's iconic goal-line interception of Russell Wilson. No players from that game remain on either roster. Josh McDaniels, now Seattle's offensive coordinator, was New England's offensive coordinator in that game -- the only coach appearing in both matchups.

The Seahawks enter as 4.5-point favorites, seeking to avenge that heartbreaking loss and win the franchise's second Super Bowl championship (their first came in Super Bowl XLVIII, a 43-8 demolition of the Broncos).

## The Russell Wilson Trade: The Gift That Keeps Giving

Of the 53-man roster that beat the 49ers in the Divisional Round, **26 players were drafted since 2022**. Of that number, **13 are starters** and a handful of others are key rotational or special-teams players. The trade haul directly produced:

| Pick | Player | Position | 2025 Role |
|------|--------|----------|-----------|
| 2022 R1 (#9) | Charles Cross | OT | Starting LT |
| 2022 R2 | Boye Mafe | EDGE | Starting DE, top-20 pass rush win rate |
| 2023 R1 (#5) | Devon Witherspoon | CB | PFF's #1 CB, 3x Pro Bowl |
| 2023 R1 (#20, traded up) | Jaxon Smith-Njigba | WR | NFL OPOY, franchise receiving record |

The Wilson trade is widely regarded as one of the most lopsided trades in modern NFL history, transforming Seattle from a declining team into a Super Bowl contender in just three years.

## Conclusion

The 2025 Seattle Seahawks represent the culmination of a masterful rebuild. The Russell Wilson trade provided the draft capital to build a young, talented core. The coaching change from Pete Carroll to Mike Macdonald unlocked a historically dominant defense. And the bold 2025 offseason moves -- trading DK Metcalf, releasing Tyler Lockett, signing Sam Darnold and Cooper Kupp -- gave the team the final pieces it needed.

Seattle enters Super Bowl LX riding a nine-game winning streak, with the NFL's best defense, the Offensive Player of the Year, and a quarterback playing the best football of his career. The ghosts of Super Bowl XLIX loom large, but this is an entirely different Seahawks team -- younger, deeper, and more balanced than the 2014 squad.

The question is whether Sam Darnold's turnover tendencies will resurface on the biggest stage, or whether this truly is his redemption story.
