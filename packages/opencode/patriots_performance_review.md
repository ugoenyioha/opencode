# New England Patriots Performance Review: 2021-2025

This report details the performance of the New England Patriots over the past five NFL seasons (2021-2025), covering their win/loss records, playoff results, key players, coaching changes, draft picks, and the overall trajectory of the post-Tom Brady era.

---

## 2021 Season: A Promising Start to the Post-Brady Era

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Record** | 10-7 (Regular Season), 10-8 (Including Playoffs) |
| **Division Finish** | 2nd in AFC East |
| **Playoffs** | Lost in AFC Wild Card to Buffalo Bills, 17-47 |
| **Head Coach** | Bill Belichick (22nd season) |
| **Offensive Coordinator** | Josh McDaniels |

### Key Players
- **Mac Jones (QB):** Selected 15th overall in the 2021 NFL Draft -- the first time the Patriots drafted a first-round quarterback since Drew Bledsoe in 1993. Jones started all 17 games as a rookie, completing 352 of 521 passes for 3,801 yards, 22 touchdowns, and 13 interceptions. He earned Pro Bowl honors and finished second in Offensive Rookie of the Year voting behind Ja'Marr Chase.
- **Damien Harris (RB):** Led the team's strong rushing attack, a key component of the offense designed to protect the rookie QB.
- **Jakobi Meyers (WR):** The team's leading receiver and most reliable target for Jones.
- **Matthew Judon (LB):** A marquee free agent acquisition who led the team in sacks and was named to the Pro Bowl. A defensive anchor.

### Draft Class
- **Round 1, Pick 15:** Mac Jones, QB, Alabama
- **Round 2, Pick 38:** Christian Barmore, DT, Alabama
- **Round 4, Pick 120:** Rhamondre Stevenson, RB, Oklahoma

### Season Highlights and Lowlights
- A seven-game winning streak in the middle of the season propelled the Patriots to the top of the AFC standings.
- The defense finished 4th in the NFL, anchoring the team's success. The team scored 462 points and allowed only 303.
- The season ended with an embarrassing 47-17 blowout loss to the Buffalo Bills in the Wild Card round. Buffalo scored a touchdown on virtually every offensive possession.

### Analysis
The 2021 season was the most optimistic year of the post-Brady era. Mac Jones looked like a legitimate franchise quarterback, the defense was elite, and the Patriots returned to the playoffs. There was real hope that New England had found its path forward.

## 2022 Season: Offensive Collapse and Regression

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Record** | 8-9 |
| **Division Finish** | 3rd in AFC East |
| **Playoffs** | Did not qualify |
| **Head Coach** | Bill Belichick (23rd season) |
| **De Facto Offensive Coordinator** | Matt Patricia |

### Key Players
- **Mac Jones (QB):** Regressed significantly in his second season, finishing with 2,997 passing yards, 14 touchdowns, and 11 interceptions. The new offensive scheme under Matt Patricia clearly hampered his development.
- **Bailey Zappe (QB):** The 2022 4th-round pick started two games and played well in relief, sparking a brief QB controversy among the fanbase.
- **Rhamondre Stevenson (RB):** Emerged as one of the best running backs in the NFL, leading the team in both rushing and receiving yards.
- **Matthew Judon (LB):** Continued his dominance on defense, leading the team in sacks for a second consecutive year and earning another Pro Bowl selection.

### Draft Class
- **Round 1, Pick 29:** Cole Strange, G, UT-Chattanooga
- **Round 2, Pick 50:** Tyquan Thornton, WR, Baylor
- **Round 4, Pick 127:** Bailey Zappe, QB, Western Kentucky

### Season Highlights and Lowlights
- The biggest storyline was the disastrous offensive coaching decision. After Josh McDaniels departed to become head coach of the Las Vegas Raiders, Belichick controversially appointed longtime defensive coach Matt Patricia as the de facto offensive coordinator and Joe Judge as QB coach -- neither had prior offensive coaching experience. The offense plummeted from 15th in yards and 6th in points scored to 26th in yards and 17th in points scored.
- The infamous "lateral" play against the Las Vegas Raiders: Jakobi Meyers attempted a reckless lateral that was intercepted by Chandler Jones and returned for a game-winning touchdown as time expired. This play became a symbol of the team's undisciplined, poorly coached offense.
- The team missed the playoffs for the second time in three seasons.

### Analysis
The 2022 season was a clear step backward. The Matt Patricia offensive experiment was a failure by every measurable standard, and Mac Jones's development stalled. Warning signs about the coaching staff's ability to adapt in the post-Brady era were becoming impossible to ignore.

## 2023 Season: Rock Bottom and the End of the Belichick Era

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Record** | 4-13 |
| **Division Finish** | 4th in AFC East (last place) |
| **Playoffs** | Did not qualify |
| **Head Coach** | Bill Belichick (24th and final season) |
| **Offensive Coordinator** | Bill O'Brien |

### Key Players
- **Mac Jones (QB):** Continued to struggle, finishing with 2,120 passing yards, 10 touchdowns, and 12 interceptions in 11 games before being benched. He was demoted multiple times -- first to backup, then to third-string by season's end, ultimately falling behind Bailey Zappe and Nathan Rourke on the depth chart. Was traded to the Jacksonville Jaguars the following offseason.
- **Bailey Zappe (QB):** Started the final six games after Jones was permanently benched, going 2-4 as a starter. Went 10-of-25 for 104 yards and one interception in his first three replacement appearances.
- **DeMario Douglas (WR):** A bright spot as a rookie 6th-round pick, leading the team in receiving yards.
- **Ezekiel Elliott (RB):** Signed in the offseason and led the team in rushing, but provided diminishing returns.

### Draft Class
- **Round 1, Pick 17:** Christian Gonzalez, CB, Oregon (strong selection)
- **Round 2, Pick 46:** Keion White, EDGE, Georgia Tech

### Season Highlights and Lowlights
- The Patriots started 1-5 for the first time since 1995 and endured a 5-game losing streak.
- They finished with the worst record in the AFC at 4-13, their worst overall record since 1992.
- The Patriots finished last in the AFC East for the first time since 2000.
- The quarterback situation was toxic: Jones was benched, Zappe was ineffective, and Belichick refused to name a starter in the final weeks.
- Bill Belichick and owner Robert Kraft mutually agreed to part ways following the season, ending a 24-year tenure that produced six Super Bowl championships. Belichick's final game was a 17-3 loss to the New York Jets.

### Analysis
The 2023 season was an unmitigated disaster and the lowest point of the franchise in over three decades. It became clear that the Mac Jones experiment had failed, the roster was devoid of offensive talent, and the Belichick era, for all its legendary accomplishments, had run its course. The one silver lining: the 4-13 record gave the Patriots the 3rd overall pick in the 2024 NFL Draft.

## 2024 Season: The Transition Year

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Record** | 4-13 |
| **Division Finish** | 4th in AFC East |
| **Playoffs** | Did not qualify (eliminated for 3rd consecutive year) |
| **Head Coach** | Jerod Mayo (1st and only season) |
| **Offensive Coordinator** | Alex Van Pelt |
| **EVP of Player Personnel** | Eliot Wolf |

### Key Players
- **Drake Maye (QB):** Selected 3rd overall in the 2024 NFL Draft. Took over as the starter in Week 6 and started 10 games, throwing for 2,276 yards, 15 touchdowns, and 10 interceptions with a 66.6% completion rate and a 3-9 record. Despite the team's struggles, Maye showed enough promise to earn a Pro Bowl selection (3rd in fan voting) and was clearly the franchise's future.
- **Jacoby Brissett (QB):** Signed as a veteran bridge starter, began the season as QB1 before ceding the job to Maye.
- **Rhamondre Stevenson (RB):** Continued to anchor the backfield.
- **Antonio Gibson (RB):** Signed in free agency to complement Stevenson.

### Draft Class
- **Round 1, Pick 3:** Drake Maye, QB, North Carolina
- **Round 2, Pick 34:** Ja'Lynn Polk, WR, Washington

### Season Highlights and Lowlights
- The Patriots matched their AFC-worst 4-13 record from 2023.
- They started 1-6 and lost 6 straight games for the first time since 1993.
- The offense ranked 30th in points scored (289 points, 17.0 per game). The defense allowed 417 points (24.5 per game, 22nd).
- Jerod Mayo became the first Black head coach in franchise history but was fired within two hours of the final game of the regular season -- making him one of the rare NFL head coaches to last only a single season. A pre-drafted statement was released announcing his departure.

### Analysis
The 2024 season was another lost year in the standings, but it served an important developmental purpose. Drake Maye showed flashes of being a franchise quarterback, giving the organization hope for the first time since Mac Jones's rookie year. The firing of Jerod Mayo after just one season was a ruthless but decisive move that set the stage for the most consequential coaching hire since Bill Belichick arrived in 2000.

## 2025 Season: The Vrabel Renaissance

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Record** | 14-3 (Regular Season) |
| **Division Finish** | 1st in AFC East (first title since 2019) |
| **Playoffs** | Won AFC Wild Card vs. Los Angeles Chargers, 16-3; Won AFC Divisional vs. Houston Texans, 28-16; Won AFC Championship vs. Denver Broncos, 10-7 |
| **Super Bowl** | Super Bowl LX vs. Seattle Seahawks (scheduled Feb. 9, 2026) |
| **Head Coach** | Mike Vrabel (1st season) |

### Coaching Hire: Mike Vrabel
Mike Vrabel was hired on January 12, 2025, replacing the one-and-done Jerod Mayo. Vrabel, a former Patriots player who won three Super Bowls with the franchise as a linebacker, brought an immediate culture change. He was named the 2025 AP NFL Coach of the Year and PFWA Coach of the Year for leading the greatest single-season turnaround in franchise history.

### Key Offseason Moves
The Patriots entered 2025 free agency with the most salary cap space in the NFL. Vrabel and the front office executed a transformative offseason:

| Signing | Position | Contract |
|---|---|---|
| Milton Williams | DT | 4 years, $104M (highest-paid player in franchise history) |
| Carlton Davis III | CB | 3 years, $60M |
| Harold Landry III | EDGE | 3 years, $43.5M ($26M guaranteed) |
| Robert Spillane | LB | 3 years, $37M |
| Morgan Moses | RT | 3 years, $24M |
| Stefon Diggs | WR | Signed (returning from torn ACL) |
| Mack Hollins | WR | Signed |
| Austin Bradbury | C | 2 years (stabilized the O-line for Maye) |

### Draft Class
- **Round 1, Pick 4:** Will Campbell, OL
- **Round 2, Pick 38:** TreVeyon Henderson, RB, Ohio State
- Additional selections: Joshua Farmer, Bradyn Swinson

### Key Players
- **Drake Maye (QB):** Had a historic sophomore season -- 4,394 passing yards, 31 touchdowns, 8 interceptions, and a 72% completion rate. The completion percentage was the highest in Patriots history, the highest in the NFL in 2025, and the sixth-highest single-season mark in NFL history. Led the NFL in yards per attempt and completion percentage. Earned a second consecutive Pro Bowl selection, second-team All-Pro honors, and finished second in MVP voting.
- **Milton Williams (DT):** Anchored a dominant defensive line after signing the franchise-record contract.
- **Harold Landry III (EDGE):** Reunited with Vrabel from their Tennessee Titans days, providing a consistent pass rush.
- **Stefon Diggs (WR):** Successfully returned from his ACL injury to give Maye a proven weapon.
- **TreVeyon Henderson (RB):** The 2nd-round pick added a dynamic element to the backfield.

### Season Highlights
- The .824 winning percentage was the best for the franchise since the Super Bowl LI-winning 2016 season.
- The Week 17 victory over the New York Jets clinched the Patriots' first AFC East championship since 2019.
- The Patriots secured their first playoff berth since 2021.
- The AFC Championship victory over Denver (10-7) was a defensive masterpiece, sending the Patriots to Super Bowl LX against the Seattle Seahawks.
- Maye became just the eighth quarterback in NFL history to finish a season with 4,000+ passing yards, 30+ touchdowns, and a 70%+ completion percentage, joining Joe Burrow (2021), Drew Brees (2017), Tony Romo (2014), and Tom Brady (2007).

### Analysis
The 2025 season represents one of the most dramatic turnarounds in NFL history. A team that went 4-13 in consecutive seasons transformed into a 14-3 juggernaut and Super Bowl contender in a single offseason. The combination of Vrabel's leadership, the front office's aggressive free agency spending, strong draft picks, and Drake Maye's explosive development created a perfect storm. As of this writing, the Patriots are preparing to face the Seattle Seahawks in Super Bowl LX on February 9, 2026, at Levi's Stadium in Santa Clara, California.

### Coaching Changes

1. **Bill Belichick (2000-2023):** The greatest coach in NFL history by many measures (6 Super Bowl titles), Belichick's final years in New England were marked by declining roster talent, questionable coaching staff decisions (the Matt Patricia experiment), and an inability to develop a post-Brady quarterback. He and the Patriots mutually parted ways after the 4-13 2023 season.

2. **Jerod Mayo (2024):** Belichick's hand-picked successor lasted just one season, going 4-13 and being fired within hours of the final game. While the results were poor, the drafting of Drake Maye at #3 overall proved to be the most consequential move of the transition period.

3. **Mike Vrabel (2025-present):** The former Patriots linebacker returned to Foxborough and immediately transformed the franchise culture. Armed with the NFL's most cap space and a developing star in Drake Maye, Vrabel orchestrated a historic turnaround, going 14-3 and reaching Super Bowl LX in his first season. Named 2025 NFL Coach of the Year.

### Overall Trajectory

The Patriots' 2021-2025 arc tells a compelling story of decline and resurrection:

- **2021:** Hope. Mac Jones and a strong defense suggested the post-Brady transition might be smooth.
- **2022:** Cracks. The Matt Patricia offensive debacle exposed coaching dysfunction and Jones's limitations.
- **2023:** Collapse. The worst season in 30+ years ended the Belichick era and the Mac Jones experiment simultaneously.
- **2024:** Foundation. Another 4-13 season, but Drake Maye's emergence as a franchise quarterback provided the essential building block for the future.
- **2025:** Resurrection. Mike Vrabel's arrival, aggressive roster construction, and Maye's breakout combined to produce the most dramatic single-season turnaround in franchise history -- from worst in the AFC to Super Bowl contenders.

The New England Patriots' story from 2021 to 2025 is ultimately one of a franchise that had to fully bottom out before it could rebuild. The two consecutive 4-13 seasons in 2023 and 2024 were painful, but they yielded the high draft capital (Drake Maye at #3 overall), salary cap space, and organizational reset that made the 2025 renaissance possible. As the Patriots prepare for Super Bowl LX, the franchise appears to have found its next great quarterback and its next great coach -- a foundation that could sustain success for years to come.
