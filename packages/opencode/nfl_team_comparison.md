## Rebuild Strategies Compared

### Seattle Seahawks: "The Steady Climb"

The Seahawks never fully bottomed out. Their worst season was 7-10 in 2021, and they improved every year after:

- **7 -> 9 -> 9 -> 10 -> 14 wins**
- Never had a top-5 draft pick
- Built through elite mid-round drafting (Wilson trade haul) and smart free agency
- Traded away franchise icons (Wilson, Metcalf, Lockett, Smith) at peak value
- Willingness to make bold roster moves even when competitive

**Signature Move:** The Russell Wilson trade (March 2022) -- yielded Charles Cross, Boye Mafe, Devon Witherspoon, and Derick Hall, forming the core of the 2025 contender.

### New England Patriots: "The Hard Reset"

The Patriots experienced a true bottoming-out before their resurgence:

- **10 -> 8 -> 4 -> 4 -> 14 wins**
- Endured back-to-back 4-13 seasons (2023-2024)
- High draft capital yielded franchise QB Drake Maye (#3 overall, 2024)
- Massive cap space enabled a transformative 2025 free agency class
- Coaching hire of Mike Vrabel produced immediate culture change

**Signature Move:** Hiring Mike Vrabel + the 2025 free agency blitz (Milton Williams $104M, Carlton Davis $60M, Harold Landry $43.5M) that instantly upgraded the roster.

## Overall Assessment

### Which Team Had the Better 5-Year Run?

**By the numbers, Seattle was more consistent.** The Seahawks posted a .576 winning percentage over 5 years (49-36) compared to the Patriots' .471 (40-45). Seattle never had a losing season worse than 7-10 and steadily improved each year.

**New England had the more dramatic arc.** The Patriots' journey from 4-13 (twice) to 14-3 and a Super Bowl berth is one of the most remarkable turnarounds in NFL history. Their 2025 season alone arguably rivals any single season in the Seahawks' five-year window.

### Looking Ahead

Both franchises appear well-positioned for sustained success:

- **Seattle** has a proven defensive system under Macdonald, elite young talent (Smith-Njigba, Witherspoon), and a quarterback in Darnold who thrives in their system.
- **New England** has a potential franchise QB in Drake Maye (still on his rookie contract), a culture-setting coach in Vrabel, and cap flexibility going forward.

The Super Bowl LX matchup is a fitting culmination: two storied franchises that rebuilt on the fly, found new identities, and are now meeting on the biggest stage -- 11 years after their unforgettable Super Bowl XLIX clash.
