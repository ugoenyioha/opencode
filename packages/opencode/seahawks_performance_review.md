# Seattle Seahawks Performance Review (2021-2025)

## Executive Summary

The Seattle Seahawks experienced a dramatic five-year arc from 2021 to 2025, transitioning from the end of the Russell Wilson era through a rebuilding phase and emerging as one of the NFL's elite teams. After posting a losing record in 2021 (7-10), the franchise made bold moves -- trading Wilson, drafting exceptionally well, and eventually hiring a new head coach -- culminating in a 14-3 season in 2025 and a berth in Super Bowl LX.

## 2021 Season: The End of an Era

**Record: 7-10 (4th in NFC West)**
**Head Coach: Pete Carroll | OC: Shane Waldron | DC: Ken Norton**

### Overview

The 2021 season marked the end of the Seahawks' dynasty-era core. It was the first losing season under Pete Carroll since 2009, ending a streak of nine consecutive winning seasons.

### Key Events

- **Russell Wilson's Injury:** Wilson missed games for the first time in his NFL career, sitting out three games after injuring his middle finger in Week 5 against the Rams. Backup Geno Smith started in his absence.
- **Defensive Struggles:** The Seahawks ranked last in the NFL in total yards allowed, giving up 6,445 yards (379.1 per game).
- **Close Game Woes:** Seattle went 0-5 in games decided by a field goal or less and 0-2 in overtime.

### Statistical Leaders

| Category | Player | Stat |
|----------|--------|------|
| Passing Yards | Russell Wilson | 3,113 yards |
| Receiving Yards | Tyler Lockett | 1,175 yards |
| Receptions | DK Metcalf | 75 |
| Rushing Yards | Rashaad Penny | 749 yards |
| Interceptions | Quandre Diggs | 5 |
| Sacks | Carlos Dunlap | 8.5 |

### Draft Picks (2021)

- D'Wayne Eskridge (WR)
- Tre Brown (CB)

### Significance

This was the final season for Russell Wilson and Bobby Wagner (first stint) in Seattle. The franchise was clearly at a crossroads, and the offseason that followed would reshape the team's future.

## 2022 Season: The Geno Smith Resurgence

**Record: 9-8 (2nd in NFC West)**
**Head Coach: Pete Carroll | Playoff Result: Lost Wild Card vs. San Francisco 49ers, 41-23**

### The Russell Wilson Trade

On March 8, 2022, the Seahawks traded Russell Wilson to the Denver Broncos in one of the most consequential trades in franchise history.

**Seahawks received:**
- QB Drew Lock
- TE Noah Fant
- DE Shelby Harris
- 2022 1st-round pick (No. 9 overall)
- 2023 1st-round pick
- 2022 2nd-round pick (No. 40 overall)
- 2023 2nd-round pick
- 2022 5th-round pick

**Seahawks sent:**
- QB Russell Wilson
- 2022 4th-round pick

### Geno Smith's Comeback

Geno Smith won the starting job in August and proceeded to have one of the most remarkable comeback seasons in NFL history:

| Stat | Value | Note |
|------|-------|------|
| Completion % | 69.8% | Led NFL, franchise record |
| Passing Yards | 4,282 | Franchise record |
| Completions | 399 | Franchise record |
| Attempts | 572 | Franchise record |
| Touchdowns | 30+ | Third Seahawk QB to reach 30 TDs |
| Games Started | 17/17 | Only QB to play every offensive snap |

Smith won the **AP NFL Comeback Player of the Year** award and was named to the Pro Bowl.

### Draft Picks (2022)

Using their haul from the Wilson trade, the Seahawks drafted:
- **Charles Cross** (OT, 1st round) -- developed into one of the NFL's top young tackles
- **Boye Mafe** (EDGE, 2nd round) -- became an integral part of the pass rush

### Playoff Result

The Seahawks earned a Wild Card berth but fell to the San Francisco 49ers 41-23 at Levi's Stadium. Smith completed 25-of-35 passes for 253 yards, 2 TDs, and 1 INT, but also lost a fumble.

### Season Highlight

Week 1: The Seahawks hosted the Broncos in Russell Wilson's return to Seattle and won 17-16, a symbolically significant victory that set the tone for the season.

## 2023 Season: Playoff Heartbreak

**Record: 9-8 (3rd in NFC West)**
**Head Coach: Pete Carroll | Playoff Result: Missed**

### Overview

The Seahawks posted the same 9-8 record as 2022 but failed to make the playoffs due to a devastating Week 17 loss and unfavorable tiebreakers.

### Geno Smith's Performance

| Stat | Value |
|------|-------|
| Passing Yards | 3,624 |
| Touchdowns | 20 |
| Interceptions | 9 |
| Rushing Yards | 155 |
| Game-Winning Drives | 5 (tied franchise record) |

Smith threw an NFL-record seven go-ahead touchdown passes in the fourth quarter and overtime. He posted the league's best Total QBR over the final six weeks and was named to the Pro Bowl for the second consecutive season. The Seahawks became the first team since 1970 with five game-winning touchdowns in the final two minutes of regulation or overtime in a single season.

### How Playoffs Slipped Away

- The Seahawks entered Week 17 in control of their playoff destiny, needing to win their final two games.
- A 30-23 loss to the Pittsburgh Steelers in Week 17 cost them control.
- Seattle beat the Cardinals 21-20 in Week 18, but also needed the Bears to beat the Packers. Green Bay won, eliminating the Seahawks.

### Draft Picks (2023) -- The Foundation of the Future

Using the remaining picks from the Wilson trade, Seattle made two franchise-altering first-round selections:

- **Devon Witherspoon** (CB, No. 5 overall) -- Named All-Pro cornerback; became just the third defensive back in 40 years (along with Charles Woodson and Patrick Peterson) to make the Pro Bowl in each of his first three seasons.
- **Jaxon Smith-Njigba** (WR, No. 20 overall) -- Would go on to win Offensive Player of the Year in 2025.
- **Derick Hall** (EDGE, 2nd round) -- Became a starting edge rusher, selected with the final pick from the Wilson trade.

### Coaching Change

After the season, Pete Carroll and the Seahawks "mutually agreed" to part ways on January 10, 2024. Carroll ended his 14-year tenure as the winningest head coach in franchise history with a 137-89-1 regular season record and a 10-9 postseason record, including the Super Bowl XLVIII championship. Carroll remained with the organization in an advisory role.

## 2024 Season: Macdonald's Promising Debut

**Record: 10-7 (2nd in NFC West)**
**Head Coach: Mike Macdonald | Playoff Result: Missed**

### New Era Begins

On January 31, 2024, the Seahawks hired **Mike Macdonald**, the Baltimore Ravens' defensive coordinator, as the ninth head coach in franchise history. At the time of his hiring, Macdonald was the youngest head coach in the NFL.

### Season Performance

Despite a strong 10-7 record, the Seahawks missed the playoffs in a historically unusual fashion:

- **Road Dominance:** 7-1 away from home (only loss at Detroit in Week 4)
- **Home Struggles:** 3-6 at Lumen Field
- **Playoff Miss:** Finished tied with the Los Angeles Rams for the NFC West title, but lost the division on a strength-of-victory tiebreaker. Seattle became the first team since the NFL expanded to 17 games to miss the playoffs with double-digit wins.

### Key Roster Moves (2024 Offseason)

**Draft:**
- Round 1: DT Byron Murphy II (Texas)
- Round 3: G Christian Haynes (UConn)
- LB Tyrice Knight
- TE AJ Barner

**Free Agency:**
- DL Leonard Williams re-signed (3 years, $64.5M)
- LB Jerome Baker and LB Tyrel Dodson signed (replacing Jordyn Brooks and Bobby Wagner)
- OG Laken Tomlinson signed

**Departures:**
- LB Jordyn Brooks (signed with Miami)
- LB Bobby Wagner (signed with Washington)

### Significance

While the playoff miss was disappointing, the 10-7 record made Macdonald the first head coach in franchise history to reach 10 wins in his inaugural season. The defensive improvement under his leadership laid the groundwork for the 2025 breakthrough.

## 2025 Season: Return to Glory

**Record: 14-3 (1st in NFC West, 1st in NFC)**
**Head Coach: Mike Macdonald | Playoff Result: Super Bowl LX vs. New England Patriots**

### Overview

The 2025 season was a historic one for the Seattle Seahawks. They set a franchise record with 14 regular season wins, captured the No. 1 seed in the NFC for the first time since 2014, and won the NFC West for the first time since 2020.

### Major Offseason Moves (2025)

The Seahawks made bold roster moves heading into the season:

| Move | Details |
|------|---------|
| Traded Geno Smith | Sent to Las Vegas Raiders for 92nd overall pick |
| Traded DK Metcalf | Sent to Pittsburgh Steelers for 52nd overall pick |
| Released Tyler Lockett | End of a decade-long Seahawks career |
| Signed Sam Darnold | 3-year, $100.5M contract |
| Signed Cooper Kupp | 3-year, $45M contract (released by Rams) |
| Signed DeMarcus Lawrence | Free agent signing |

### Sam Darnold's Career-Best Season

| Stat | Regular Season | Playoffs |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Passing Yards | 4,048 | 470 |
| Completion % | 67.7% | 69.8% (37/53) |
| Touchdowns | 25 | 4 |
| Interceptions | 14 | 0 |
| Passer Rating | 99.1 | -- |
| Record | 14-3 | 2-0 |

Darnold became just the second quarterback in NFL history (along with Tom Brady) to post a 14-3 record.

### Jaxon Smith-Njigba: Offensive Player of the Year

Smith-Njigba had a historic third-year breakout:

- **119 receptions** for **1,793 receiving yards** (both franchise records)
- Led the NFL in receiving yards
- Named unanimous first-team All-Pro
- Won **AP Offensive Player of the Year** -- just the second Seahawk to win the award (after Shaun Alexander in 2005)
- NFC Championship: 10 receptions, 153 yards, 1 TD in the 31-27 victory over the Rams

### Elite Defense

The Seahawks' defense was the NFL's best in 2025:

- **NFL-low 292 points allowed**
- **League-best point differential: +191**
- First time the Seahawks led the NFL in scoring defense since the "Legion of Boom" era
- **Devon Witherspoon** continued his All-Pro trajectory, earning his third consecutive Pro Bowl selection

### Playoff Run

| Round | Opponent | Result |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Divisional Round | San Francisco 49ers | **W 41-6** |
| NFC Championship | Los Angeles Rams | **W 31-27** |
| Super Bowl LX | New England Patriots | Feb. 8, 2026 (scheduled) |

The NFC Championship victory over the Rams was the Seahawks' first conference title since 2014 and sent them to Super Bowl LX -- a rematch of Super Bowl XLIX from the 2014 season.

### Macdonald's Record

Mike Macdonald became the first Seahawks coach with 10+ wins in each of his first two seasons, cementing himself as one of the NFL's top young coaches.

### The Russell Wilson Trade: A Franchise-Altering Deal

The March 2022 trade of Russell Wilson to Denver is arguably the most impactful trade in recent Seahawks history. While Wilson struggled in Denver (the Broncos went 5-12 in 2022, leading to coach Nathaniel Hackett's firing), the Seahawks used the draft capital to build a championship-caliber roster:

### Coaching Transition

- **Pete Carroll (2010-2023):** 137-89-1 regular season, 10-9 postseason, 1 Super Bowl win. Departed after 14 seasons as the franchise's all-time winningest coach.
- **Mike Macdonald (2024-present):** 24-10 in two regular seasons. Defensive mastermind who built the NFL's top-ranked defense in 2025 and led the team to Super Bowl LX.

### Overall Assessment

The 2021-2025 period represents one of the most successful rebuilds in modern NFL history. The Seahawks went from a 7-10 team losing its franchise quarterback to a 14-3 powerhouse heading to the Super Bowl in just four years. The keys to this transformation were:

1. **Masterful drafting** -- particularly the 2022-2023 classes built on Wilson trade capital
2. **Bold roster turnover** -- willingness to move on from franchise icons (Wilson, Metcalf, Lockett, Smith)
3. **Coaching upgrade** -- Macdonald's defensive expertise elevated the entire organization
4. **Strategic free agency** -- the Darnold and Kupp signings in 2025 provided the final pieces

The Seahawks enter Super Bowl LX on February 8, 2026, against the New England Patriots in a rematch of their iconic Super Bowl XLIX matchup from the 2014 season.
