# Super Bowl LX Prediction: Seattle Seahawks vs. New England Patriots

**February 8, 2026 | 6:30 PM ET | Levi's Stadium, Santa Clara, CA | NBC**
**Line: Seahawks -4.5 | O/U: 45.5**

---

## Five-Year Context: Two Franchises Reborn

### Seattle Seahawks: From the Ashes of the Legion of Boom

The Seahawks' path to Super Bowl LX begins with the day they traded Russell Wilson to Denver on March 8, 2022. What looked like a franchise-gutting move turned out to be one of the most lopsided trades in modern NFL history. The haul produced four starters who now form the spine of this team: left tackle Charles Cross, edge rusher Boye Mafe, PFF's top-ranked cornerback Devon Witherspoon, and the 2025 NFL Offensive Player of the Year, Jaxon Smith-Njigba.

After two 9-8 seasons under Pete Carroll, Seattle hired the 36-year-old defensive savant Mike Macdonald from Baltimore. His first year (2024) produced 10 wins but a heartbreaking playoff miss on the fifth tiebreaker. The 2025 offseason was audacious: they traded DK Metcalf, released Tyler Lockett, signed Sam Darnold on a prove-it deal, and brought Cooper Kupp home to Washington state. The result was a franchise-record 14-3 season, the NFC's No. 1 seed for the first time since 2014, and a nine-game winning streak into the Super Bowl.

### New England Patriots: The Dynasty Reawakens

The Patriots' journey was more agonizing. After Tom Brady's departure, they endured Mac Jones' rise and fall, Matt Patricia's offensive coordinator disaster, the Belichick era's sad finale at 4-13, and Jerod Mayo's one-and-done catastrophe that produced another 4-13 season. Five years, three quarterbacks, three coaches, and a fanbase wondering if the dynasty was truly dead.

Then Mike Vrabel came home. The three-time Super Bowl champion as a Patriots player brought Josh McDaniels back as offensive coordinator, spent over $200 million in free agency (Milton Williams, Carlton Davis, Harold Landry, Stefon Diggs, Robert Spillane), and unlocked Drake Maye's full potential. The result was a 10-win improvement -- tied for the greatest turnaround in NFL history -- a 14-3 record, the AFC East title, and the AP Coach of the Year award for Vrabel. The dynasty was just sleeping.

## Matchup Analysis

### Quarterback Matchup: Sam Darnold vs. Drake Maye

This is a tale of two redemptions at wildly different stages.

**Sam Darnold** (Age 28): The former No. 3 overall pick who bounced from the Jets to the Panthers to the Vikings before finding himself in Seattle. His regular season was a study in contradiction: 4,048 yards, 25 TDs, but 14 interceptions. His first half was brilliant (16 TDs, 5 INTs); his second half was alarming (9 TDs, 9 INTs). But in the playoffs, Darnold has been a different quarterback entirely: 37-of-53, 470 yards, 4 TDs, 0 INTs across two games. A 122.4 passer rating. Zero turnovers. He has been the postseason's best quarterback by virtually every metric.

**Drake Maye** (Age 23): The second-year phenom out of North Carolina who may be the most talented quarterback in this game. His 2025 regular season was historic: 4,394 yards, 31 TDs, 8 INTs, a 72.0% completion rate (highest in franchise history, sixth-best in NFL history), and the league's best QBR (77.1). He finished one vote shy of NFL MVP. But the playoffs have been rougher: 43-of-77 (55.8%), 533 yards, 4 TDs, 2 INTs, and six fumbles (three lost). The raw talent is elite; the postseason execution has been inconsistent.

**Edge: Even.** Maye is the more talented passer with the higher ceiling. Darnold is playing the best football of his life at the right time. Maye's playoff struggles and fumbling issues give this matchup a push despite his superior regular-season numbers.

### Offensive Comparison

**Seattle's Offense:**
The Seahawks' passing game runs through Jaxon Smith-Njigba, the NFL's Offensive Player of the Year (119 catches, 1,793 yards, 10 TDs, 542 deep receiving yards -- all of which led the NFL). JSN is a route-running technician who averaged 3.68 yards per route run, the third-best mark of any player in the past decade. Cooper Kupp (47 catches, 593 yards, 2 TDs) provides a reliable veteran complement and came up huge in the NFC Championship with the go-ahead touchdown. Kenneth Walker III (1,027 yards, 4.6 YPC) gives Seattle a credible ground game, and his late-season surge (5.3 YPC over his last five games including playoffs) suggests he is peaking at the right time.

**New England's Offense:**
The Patriots' offense revolves around Maye's arm talent and the running game. Stefon Diggs (85 catches, 1,013 yards, 4 TDs) is the first Patriots receiver to top 1,000 yards since Julian Edelman in 2019, but beyond Diggs, the receiving corps is thin. Kayshon Boutte disappears in big moments. The running game features Rhamondre Stevenson (603 yards) and rookie TreVeyon Henderson (911 yards, 9 TDs, 5.4 YPC), who has been electric. The offense ranked 2nd in scoring (28.8 PPG) and 3rd in total yards (379.4 YPG) during the regular season -- but has managed just 16, 28, and 10 points in three playoff games.

**Edge: Seattle.** The Seahawks have the NFL's best receiver, a more balanced attack, and have scored more consistently in the playoffs (41 and 31 points). New England's offensive postseason struggles -- particularly the 10-point output in Denver -- are a real concern.

### Defensive Comparison

**Seattle's Defense:**
This is the crown jewel. Mike Macdonald has built the NFL's No. 1 scoring defense (17.2 PPG), a unit that generates a league-best 40.4% pressure rate while being one of the lightest blitzing defenses in the NFL. Devon Witherspoon is PFF's top-ranked cornerback. Leonard Williams and Byron Murphy III combined for 108 QB pressures and 14.0 sacks among 300-pound players. The run defense is suffocating (2nd in the NFL at 88.8 yards allowed per game). The unit's 4.7% explosive-play differential is the best in the league and ninth-best of any team in 25 years. In two playoff games, they held opponents to 6 and 27 points.

**New England's Defense:**
The $200M+ free agency investment produced the NFL's 4th-ranked scoring defense (18.8 PPG), 4th in pass defense (262.3 yards allowed), and 6th in rush defense (128.9 yards allowed). Christian Gonzalez is a legitimate shutdown corner. Carlton Davis provides elite man coverage opposite him. Milton Williams anchors the interior. In three playoff games, they held opponents to 3, 16, and 7 points -- a staggering 8.7 PPG average that is actually more dominant than Seattle's playoff defensive numbers.

**Edge: Seattle, narrowly.** Both defenses are elite. Seattle's regular-season numbers were slightly better (1st vs. 4th in scoring defense), and their pass rush is more scheme-diverse. But New England's playoff defensive performance (8.7 PPG allowed) is a masterpiece. This is the closest matchup on the field.

### Special Teams

Seattle holds the advantage here, with return specialist Rashid Shaheed providing a dynamic element (he opened the divisional round with a kickoff return touchdown against the 49ers). The Seahawks' special teams have been a field-position weapon all season. New England's special teams are serviceable but not a difference-maker, with young players on rookie contracts at punter and kicker.

**Edge: Seattle.**

### Coaching: Mike Macdonald vs. Mike Vrabel

**Mike Macdonald** (Age 38): A defensive genius who took Seattle's defense from 30th in EPA allowed (2023) to 1st in scoring (2025) in two years. His scheme disguises and pressure packages are the best in football. But this is his first Super Bowl as a head coach, and only his second year leading a team.

**Mike Vrabel** (Age 50): A former Patriots legend with three Super Bowl rings as a player, the 2025 AP Coach of the Year, and the architect of the greatest turnaround in NFL history. His big-game pedigree is unmatched by any active coach. Josh McDaniels, his offensive coordinator, also served as Seattle's OC this season (in a delicious irony) -- wait, correction: McDaniels is New England's OC. Vrabel's coaching staff combines institutional knowledge with modern innovation.

**Edge: Vrabel, slightly.** Macdonald is the better schematic mind. But Vrabel's Super Bowl experience as both a player and coach, his ability to prepare teams for the biggest moment, and the motivational narrative of returning the franchise to glory give him a small edge in a game where coaching matters immensely.

## Key Factors That Will Decide the Game

### 1. Seattle's Elite Defense vs. Drake Maye's Arm Talent

This is THE matchup of Super Bowl LX. Macdonald's defense generates pressure without blitzing, which has historically been kryptonite for young quarterbacks. Maye's ability to extend plays and throw on the move is his greatest weapon, but his six fumbles in three playoff games suggest he is holding the ball too long under pressure. If Seattle's front four can win their individual battles -- and they have the personnel to do it -- Maye could be in for a long afternoon.

Counter-argument: Maye's 72% completion rate and ability to make off-platform throws are unlike anything Seattle has faced. He led the NFL in completion percentage over expected (+9%). He does not need to hold the ball long if the quick game is clicking.

### 2. New England's Defensive Grit vs. Darnold's Turnover Risk

The Patriots held three playoff opponents to 8.7 PPG. That is not a fluke -- it is a defensive identity built on Christian Gonzalez's lockdown coverage, Milton Williams' interior disruption, and a team that takes the ball away. Darnold threw 14 interceptions in the regular season, including 9 in the second half. His playoff form has been immaculate (0 INTs), but the small sample of two games does not erase a full season of turnover-prone play.

If the Patriots can force Darnold into 2-3 turnovers, they can win this game. If Darnold plays clean football, their offense may not be able to keep pace.

### 3. Jaxon Smith-Njigba vs. the Patriots Secondary

JSN is the best receiver in football right now. His 3.68 yards per route run is an absurd number. The Patriots will likely deploy Christian Gonzalez on JSN, but the Seahawks will motion JSN frequently to create mismatches -- forcing nickel corner Marcus Jones into one-on-one situations or pulling zone coverage apart. Expect Seattle to scheme up at least one or two explosive plays to JSN early.

The Patriots' plan will be to double-team JSN and dare Darnold to beat them with Kupp, Walker, and the secondary targets. If JSN is held under 80 yards, New England has a chance. If he goes for 120+, Seattle probably wins.

### 4. Playoff Momentum and Path

Seattle's path was the more dominant one: a 41-6 annihilation of the 49ers and a 31-27 NFC Championship win. They are scoring points and playing with confidence.

New England's path was more workmanlike: 16-3, 28-16, 10-7. They have won with defense and grit, but the offense has not consistently shown its regular-season explosiveness. The 10-7 win in Denver was a survival game. Can they suddenly turn on the scoring against the NFL's best defense?

## Historical Parallels

### The Super Bowl XLIX Rematch

On February 1, 2015, the Patriots defeated the Seahawks 28-24 in Super Bowl XLIX when Malcolm Butler intercepted Russell Wilson's pass at the goal line with 20 seconds remaining. It remains one of the most famous plays in NFL history. No players from that game remain on either roster, but the narrative weight is immense. Josh McDaniels, then New England's offensive coordinator, is now New England's offensive coordinator again -- the only coach involved in both games.

In the history of Super Bowl rematches, the team that won the first meeting is 8-3 in the second. But revenge teams have pulled it off -- most recently, the Eagles avenged their Super Bowl LVII loss to the Chiefs by winning Super Bowl LIX. The Seahawks have been waiting 11 years for this moment, and that kind of institutional memory, even without the original players, fuels a franchise.

### Historical Pattern: Dominant Defense Wins Super Bowls

History strongly favors the team with the better defense in the Super Bowl. The No. 1 scoring defense has a strong track record in the big game, and Seattle's unit fits the mold of championship-caliber defenses: they generate pressure without over-blitzing, they have an elite secondary, and they do not give up explosive plays. The 2013 Seahawks, the 2015 Broncos, and the 2000 Ravens all won Super Bowls on the strength of historically dominant defenses.

### The No. 1 Seed Advantage

Seattle is the NFC's No. 1 seed; New England is the AFC's No. 2 seed. The No. 1 overall seed in each conference has a strong historical winning percentage in the Super Bowl, particularly when the gap in seeding is narrow but the home-field advantage throughout the playoffs has produced peak form and confidence.

## The Prediction

### Seattle Seahawks 24, New England Patriots 17

**Winner: Seattle Seahawks**

This game will be closer than the 4.5-point spread suggests for most of four quarters, but Seattle's defensive superiority will ultimately be the difference.

**How it plays out:**

The first half is a defensive chess match. Both defenses assert themselves early. Macdonald's scheme disguises confuse Maye into a pair of three-and-outs in the first quarter. Seattle takes a 7-3 lead on a Darnold-to-Walker touchdown after a methodical 12-play drive. New England answers with an Andy Borregales field goal after a promising drive stalls when Gonzalez breaks up a deep shot to Diggs in the end zone. Seattle adds a field goal before halftime. It is 10-3 Seahawks at the break.

The third quarter is where the game tilts. Maye settles in and leads a beautiful touchdown drive, hitting Diggs on a slant for a 14-yard score. The game is tied 10-10 with 5:12 left in the third. But on the ensuing Seahawks drive, Darnold uncorks a 52-yard bomb to Jaxon Smith-Njigba, who beats Marcus Jones on a double move down the left sideline. Two plays later, Walker punches it in from the 3-yard line. Seattle leads 17-10.

The fourth quarter is where Seattle's defense locks the door. The Patriots manage one more scoring drive -- a gutsy 11-play series capped by a Henderson 1-yard plunge to tie it 17-17 with 8:41 remaining. But Darnold answers immediately, hitting Kupp on back-to-back plays for 18 and 22 yards, then finding JSN on a crossing route for a 16-yard touchdown that puts Seattle ahead 24-17 with 4:53 left.

On the final Patriots drive, Maye pushes the ball downfield, converting a 3rd-and-8 with a scramble. But facing 2nd-and-6 from the Seattle 38, Leonard Williams (playing through a knee issue all game) is replaced by a fresh Boye Mafe, who beats his man clean and forces Maye to throw off his back foot. Devon Witherspoon -- PFF's top-ranked corner, who has been shadowing Diggs all game -- undercuts the out route and intercepts the pass at the 31-yard line with 1:47 remaining. Seattle kneels it out.

**Final Score: Seahawks 24, Patriots 17**

### MVP: Jaxon Smith-Njigba

7 catches, 142 yards, 1 touchdown. The 52-yard reception that broke the third-quarter tie is the play of the game. JSN becomes the fourth wide receiver to win Super Bowl MVP in the last six years, cementing his status as the NFL's best receiver.

### Key Play That Decides the Game

Devon Witherspoon's interception of Drake Maye with 1:47 remaining. Witherspoon reads Maye's eyes, jumps the out route to Diggs, and seals the game. It is a fitting bookend to Super Bowl XLIX: 11 years ago, a Patriots cornerback's interception ended Seattle's championship dreams. This time, a Seahawks cornerback's interception completes the franchise's redemption.

### Confidence Level: MEDIUM-HIGH

I give this a 65% confidence rating. The 4.5-point spread is about right. Seattle's defensive advantage is the clearest edge in this game, and Darnold's playoff form has been legitimately elite. But Drake Maye is a special talent who could flip this game with two or three plays, and Vrabel's coaching in big moments cannot be discounted. This is not a slam-dunk pick -- but the better defense, the better receiving corps, and the home-state advantage for Seattle (playing in Santa Clara) push me firmly to the Seahawks.

## Alternative Scenario: How the Patriots Win

**Patriots 20, Seahawks 16**

The Patriots win this game if three things happen:

1. **Maye protects the football.** Zero turnovers. He takes what the defense gives him, checks down to Henderson and Stevenson, and does not try to force balls into tight windows against Witherspoon and the Seattle secondary. Maye plays his most disciplined game of the playoffs.

2. **The Patriots force Darnold turnovers.** Darnold's 14 regular-season interceptions are not ancient history. If Christian Gonzalez can bait Darnold into one of those second-half-of-the-season decision-making lapses -- particularly in the red zone -- the Patriots can flip the field and keep the score low. Two Darnold turnovers that lead to 10 points is the Patriots' formula.

3. **They win the ground game.** Henderson and Stevenson combine for 120+ rushing yards, keeping Maye in manageable down-and-distance situations and keeping Seattle's pass rush from teeing off. If the Patriots can sustain drives of 10+ plays, they can keep JSN off the field and control the clock.

In this scenario, it is a 13-10 game entering the fourth quarter. Maye hits Diggs on a 34-yard strike to take a 17-13 lead. Darnold, pressing, throws an interception to Carlton Davis at the Patriots' 40. Borregales kicks a 47-yard field goal. 20-13. Darnold leads a furious late drive but settles for a field goal with 34 seconds left, and New England survives 20-16. Maye wins MVP with 237 yards, 1 TD, 0 INTs, and the two biggest completions of the game.

This is a realistic path. It is just not the most likely one.

## Final Thoughts

Super Bowl LX is a collision of two of the greatest rebuild stories in NFL history. Both teams were bad -- really bad -- within the last three years. Both found their franchise quarterback, their coach, and their identity in the span of a single offseason. Both are playing in their first Super Bowl with entirely new rosters and coaching staffs.

But football games are won in the trenches, and Seattle's defensive front is the best in the NFL. Mike Macdonald's ability to generate pressure without blitzing will limit Maye's ability to create the explosive plays that define his game. Jaxon Smith-Njigba will get his, because nobody has been able to stop him all year. And Sam Darnold -- the quarterback nobody believed in for seven years -- will deliver the game of his life when it matters most.

Eleven years after Malcolm Butler's interception stole a championship from Seattle, Devon Witherspoon's interception will deliver one back.

**Pick: Seattle Seahawks 24, New England Patriots 17**
**MVP: Jaxon Smith-Njigba**
